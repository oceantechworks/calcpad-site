1. What you can do with CalcPad

CalcPad works as an everyday calculator and also lets you save the numbers and formulas you use often. You can call them up again whenever you need them. In this guide, saved formulas may also be called “templates.”

  • Calculate with arithmetic operators, parentheses, percentages, and powers
  • Recall your latest result with ANS and earlier results from HISTORY
  • Add values to or subtract values from memory to keep a running total
  • Save frequently used numbers as presets and repeated calculations as formulas
  • Use functions and constants (some functions and backup and restore require PRO).

Try a regular calculation first. Then use ANS or HISTORY to bring back the result.

Importing samples on first launch

If “Import sample data?” appears when you first open the app, choose “Import” to add 2 formulas and 3 presets. Choose “Skip” to start empty instead. Both options are available in Free.

The samples include formulas such as BMI and presets for centimeters/inches, standard gravity g, and the golden ratio φ. You can edit or delete any sample after importing it.

2. Using the calculator

2.1 Entering and calculating

  1. Tap the numbers and operators in order. For example, enter 1200 × 5 × 80%.
  2. Tap = (with ENTER underneath).
  3. The expression and result, 4800, appear in the display’s Log.

80% means 0.8. 200 + 10% equals 200.1. To increase 200 by 10%, enter 200 × (1 + 10%).

2.2 Correcting your input

  • Tap the input line to position the cursor. You can also move it left or right with ◀/▶.
  • ⌫ deletes immediately before the cursor.
  • Tap C/AC briefly to clear the expression you are entering. On the regular calculator screen, press and hold it to clear both the input and the on-screen Log.
  • C/AC does not clear ANS, HISTORY, or memory.

2.3 Parentheses, powers, and continuing a calculation

You can enter expressions such as (2 + 3) × 4 = 20 and 2 ^ 3 = 8. Multiplication and division are evaluated before addition and subtraction. To use a result in another calculation, recall it with ANS instead of typing it again.

2.4 Reusing the Log

The Log shows the steps of calculations made on the current screen. Long-press a completed expression line to insert it where the cursor is in the input line. For example, you can recall 100×1.1+500, change 100 to 200, and calculate again.

Long-press a result line to insert the result as a confirmed value in parentheses. Even if the number appears rounded on screen, CalcPad reuses the original calculation value. You can also press and hold a confirmed variable line in a formula (template). Only the number is inserted, without its variable name or unit.

Pressing and holding inserts the content into CalcPad’s input line rather than copying it to your device’s clipboard. To calculate, make any needed edits and then tap =. Clearing the Log does not remove entries from HISTORY.

3. Using ANS

ANS keeps the result of the last calculation that finished successfully. This can be a regular calculation or the final result of a saved formula. If a calculation fails, or you are still entering the variables for a formula, the previous ANS value stays available.

3.1 Recalling the latest result

  1. Enter 12 × 8 and tap = to get 96.
  2. Tap ANS normally. A green value in parentheses is inserted at the cursor.
  3. Continue with ÷ 3 and tap = to get 32.

Tap ANS to insert the value currently stored there. If ANS changes later, a value you have already inserted stays the same. Even when the display is shortened or rounded, the stored calculation value is used.

3.2 Keeping, updating, and clearing ANS

  • If ANS does not have a result yet, a short tap inserts nothing. A calculation result of zero is stored normally.
  • If it was saved successfully, your ANS value is still there when you reopen the app.
  • When another calculation finishes successfully, ANS is updated to the new result. There is no separate button for clearing ANS alone.
  • Deleting HISTORY or using MC does not clear ANS. ANS is not included in backups.

ANS opens HISTORY when you long-press it. A short tap inserts the ANS value instead.

4. Using HISTORY

HISTORY automatically saves the expressions and results of completed calculations. It holds up to 30 entries; when the list is full, the oldest entry is removed first. Regular calculations and the final results of saved formulas are included. Partially entered variables and actions that only insert a value are not recorded.

HISTORY keeps each expression as it appeared when calculated. Changing decimal places, rounding, decimal or grouping separators, or the app language later does not change a recorded expression. For formulas (templates), HISTORY shows the expression with the values used for its variables, along with the formula’s name at the time.

For example, with 6 decimal places and rounding to the nearest value, 1÷3 displays 0.333333. Insert ANS and calculate (0.333333)×3, and the result is 1. HISTORY keeps the expressions as 1÷3 and (0.333333)×3. It does not replace them with long internal numbers or add ≒ just because the displayed result was rounded. If a nonzero value is shown as zero, however, the ≒ 0 indicator remains.

Results in HISTORY are displayed using the decimal places and rounding method from the original calculation. Only the decimal and grouping separators follow your current settings. A rounded display does not mean the stored calculation value was rounded to the same number of digits. The more precise stored value is used when you recall it.

4.1 Recalling an earlier result

  1. Long-press ANS on the home screen. HISTORY appears underneath the main label.
  2. The newest entries are at the bottom. Scroll up to see older entries.
  3. Tap the expression or result area of the entry you want.
  4. Once the value has been inserted, the calculator screen opens again with the confirmed value in parentheses at the cursor.

You recall the saved result, not the expression itself. This does not reopen an editable expression or run the calculation again. Simply inserting a result does not change ANS, HISTORY, the Log, or memory.

4.2 Actions on each entry

ActionWhat it does
M+/M-Adds this entry’s result to memory or subtracts it from memory. These use a different value from M+/M- on the home screen.

4.3 Clearing and keeping history

To clear the whole list, tap the button at the top, check the “Clear all history?” message, and choose “Clear.” Deleted history cannot be restored, so review the confirmation before continuing. Clearing one entry or the whole list leaves ANS and memory unchanged.

When saved successfully, HISTORY is available the next time you open the app. It is not included in backups. For help with save failures, see Problems saving or loading data.

5. Using memory

Memory lets you add and subtract calculation results while keeping a running total. It starts at zero and is shown as M = … at the top of the home screen and the bottom of HISTORY.

KeyAction on the home screen
MCResets memory to exact zero.
MRInserts the current memory value at the cursor without clearing memory.
M+Adds the most recent successful calculation result (ANS).
M-Subtracts the most recent successful calculation result (ANS).

5.1 Adding 100 and 30

  1. Tap MC to set memory to zero.
  2. Enter 100, tap =, then M+. Memory is now 100.
  3. Enter 30, tap =, then M+. Memory is now 130.
  4. Tap MR to insert 130 into the input line. If ANS is still 30, pressing M- makes memory 100.
Check which value M+/M- will use

On the home screen, M+ and M- use the result in ANS, not the number you are currently typing. For example, typing 100 and pressing M+ will not necessarily add 100. To add 100, first complete that calculation with =. If ANS has no result yet, memory stays unchanged. While entering variables for a saved formula, M+/M- also use the last completed result.

5.2 Memory actions in HISTORY

M+/M- on a history entry use that entry’s result. In the bottom bar, tap the M = … area to the left of MC to insert memory, just as with MR, and return to the calculator. MC clears only memory to zero and keeps HISTORY open.

Adding or subtracting from memory, or clearing it with MC, does not change the expression you are typing, ANS, or HISTORY. MR simply inserts the memory value; it does not create a new calculation entry on its own. If saved successfully, memory stays available after restarting the app, but it is not included in backups. If you see M = —, memory is unavailable; that is different from a value of zero.

6. Using presets

Presets let you save numbers you often use, such as unit prices, tax rates, and conversion factors, under their own names. They are handy when you want to recall the same value repeatedly, rather than add values together in memory.

6.1 Saving a preset

  1. Tap PRE to open “Presets.”
  2. Tap ＋ at the top right to add one.
  3. Enter a value and an optional label, then save. For example: label “Hourly rate,” value 1500.

To keep a calculation result as a preset, open its three-dot menu in HISTORY and choose “Save as preset”. You do not need to type in the rounded number shown on the screen. The original result is saved, and you can give it a new name.

6.2 Recalling and editing presets

  • Open PRE and tap an entry to insert it at the cursor as a green confirmed value in parentheses.
  • Use an entry’s three-dot menu to choose “Edit” or “Delete.”
  • Choose “Reorder,” drag entries into order, and tap “Done” to return to the normal view.

Free allows up to 5 presets, including the 3 first-launch samples. The current samples are “cm / inch: 2.54,” “Standard gravity g: 9.80665,” and “Golden Ratio (φ): 1.618.” Enter π from FUNC rather than from a preset.

Presets are included in backups. If the app’s sample data is updated, presets you have already saved will not be replaced automatically.

7. Creating formulas

If you often make the same kind of calculation, you can save it as a formula. Next time, just enter the numbers that change, such as unit price and quantity. Let’s create a formula for “Unit price × Quantity × 1.1.”

  1. Tap ƒ(x) to open “Formulas,” then tap ＋ at the top right.
  2. Under “Basic Information,” enter a name such as “Total including tax” in “Formula name (required).” The description is optional.
  3. Under “Calculation Result Settings,” choose the result label, decimal places, and rounding method.
  4. Use the add button under “Variables” to register “Unit price” (key a) and “Quantity” (key b), both with the Number type.
  5. In “Expression,” use the variable buttons, operators, and numbers to build a × b × 1.1.
  6. Tap “Save.”

7.1 What is a variable?

A variable is a value you fill in each time you use a saved formula. For example, you can name a variable “Unit price” and give it the key a in the expression. Setting an initial value makes it available as a suggestion when entering numbers. To save a formula, register at least one variable and use at least one of them in the expression. You do not need to use every registered variable.

Choose either Number or Percent (%). A percent variable is inserted into an expression as a%, for example. When you enter 10 while running the formula, CalcPad treats it as 10%. You do not need to divide it by 100 again in the expression.

Lowercase e is reserved for Euler's number and cannot be used as a variable key.

7.2 Functions, rounding, and editing

The function button in the expression editor is labeled “Func.” E, π, and e are available in Free; other functions require PRO. Each formula saves its own angle unit for trigonometric functions.

A formula’s result is finalized using the decimal places you choose

The decimal places and rounding method you choose for a formula apply to the final calculation value itself. This is different from simply changing how many digits appear on the home screen. ANS and HISTORY receive this finalized value too.

You can edit or delete a formula using its three-dot menu, or change its position with “Reorder”. If you want to remove a variable used in a formula, first remove it from the expression. Deleted content cannot be recovered, so check before confirming.

8. Running saved formulas

  1. Tap ƒ(x) to open the formula list, then tap the formula you want.
  2. Enter a value for the displayed variable and confirm with =/ENTER.
  3. When the next variable appears, enter and confirm its value in the same way.
  4. After you confirm the last variable, the result appears.

For “Unit price × Quantity × 1.1,” a unit price of 1200 and quantity of 3 give a result of 3960.

[Unit price] = 1200
[Quantity] = 3
[Result] = 3960

You can also do calculations such as 1200 × 2 in a variable input field, or insert values from ANS, MR, presets, and HISTORY. Values cannot be inserted while input is unavailable, such as after the final result has appeared.

Once a saved formula finishes successfully, its result is stored in ANS and recorded in HISTORY with the formula name. Entering only some of the variables does not update ANS. To stop entering values and return to the regular calculator, press and hold C/AC.

9.1 Scientific notation E and the constants π and e

  • 1.2E3 means 1.2 × 10^3 = 1200, while 1.2E-3 means 0.0012. Enter E after a number and then type the exponent. If E has no number or the exponent is unfinished, the expression cannot be calculated.
  • π is pi. The area of a circle with radius 5 is π × 5 ^ 2, approximately 78.539816 (shown to 6 decimal places).
  • Lowercase e is Euler’s number, approximately 2.718282. It differs from uppercase E in scientific notation.

9.3 min/max and commas

min and max need at least two values. On the regular calculator, , NEXT ARG appears at the lower right of the display when the cursor is inside a min or max argument list. Tap it where a separator can be inserted to add a comma and a space. When creating a saved formula, use the comma button in the expression builder.

Here, a comma separates values passed to a function; it is not a thousands separator. For example, enter max(100, 200). An expression such as max(100, ) is not complete because the last value is missing.

9.4 Practical examples

If you have 13 items and each box holds 6, you can find the number of boxes you need with ceil(13 ÷ 6) = 3.

Suppose the base fee is 3600, with an extra 100 for each kilometer beyond 10 km. You can use 3600 + 100 × max(0, a - 10), where a is the distance. When a is 8, the fee is 3600; when a is 15, it is 4100.

To give a 20% discount with a maximum discount of 1000, use min(a × 20%, 1000), where a is the price. A price of 3000 gives a discount of 600; a price of 8000 gives 1000.

9.5 Angle units and behavior after input

On the regular calculator, choose DEG (degrees) or RAD (radians) in the function sheet. DEG/RAD appears at the top of the home screen when an expression contains a trigonometric function. Each saved formula uses its own angle unit, regardless of the regular calculator’s setting.

“Auto-close after input” is available with both Free and PRO. On the regular calculator, turning it ON closes the function sheet after an insertion succeeds; turning it OFF keeps the sheet open. The formula builder’s function sheet closes after a successful insertion.

10. Calculation precision and display settings

10.1 Calculated values and displayed values are different

CalcPad aims to calculate as accurately as possible for values that can be expressed as integers, decimals, or fractions. For example, 0.1 + 0.2 gives 0.3. Some types of calculations still involve approximations.

When you finish a regular calculation, CalcPad keeps up to 30 significant digits in the result. If rounding changes the value, the result is treated as approximate. Trigonometric functions and logarithms use approximations based on 15 significant digits. π and e are also represented using a limited number of digits. CalcPad preserves the distinction between exact and approximate values when you reuse them.

Green values in parentheses inserted from ANS, MR, HISTORY, or presets may contain more digits than are shown on screen. If a confirmed value is displayed in ordinary decimal notation, you can edit its digits directly. When you do, the green formatting and automatically added parentheses are removed, and the value becomes ordinary input. Any hidden digits from the original confirmed value are no longer retained. Confirmed values shown in scientific notation (E) or containing the symbol ≒ cannot be edited digit by digit. To change one, delete the entire block and enter the value again.

10.2 Calculator display settings

  1. Open the settings icon at the top right of the home screen.
  2. Under “Calculator display settings,” choose “Decimal places,” “Rounding,” “Decimal separator,” and the grouping options.

You can set the display to 0–12 decimal places. Choose a dot or comma for the decimal separator, and no separator, a comma, a dot, or a space for digit grouping. You can group digits in sets of 3 or 4. Unnecessary zeros at the end are hidden.

For example, with 2 decimal places selected, calculating 1 ÷ 8 displays 0.13. The calculation value is still 0.125, so ANS × 8 gives 1. Changing display settings does not change the values saved in ANS, HISTORY, or memory.

10.3 Comparing rounding methods

These examples round to 1 decimal place.

Rounding1.25-1.25
Round (ties away from zero)1.3-1.3
Round down (toward smaller values)1.2-1.3
Round up (toward larger values)1.3-1.2

For negative numbers, “Round down” still rounds toward the smaller number; it does not simply remove the decimal part. The round, floor, and ceil functions round values to integers within an expression, rather than changing only what is displayed.

Each formula’s “Calculation Result Settings” round the calculation value itself when the formula finishes. Memory addition and subtraction also keep up to 30 significant digits after each operation. If you type in a number as rounded on screen, it may differ from the original calculation value.

When a small, nonzero number appears as zero because of the display setting, CalcPad shows ≒ 0. Very large numbers may appear in scientific notation. The ≒ symbol is not shown for every approximate value.

11. Backup and restore

Backup and restore are PRO features. Open Settings → “Backup & restore.”

11.1 What is and is not included

  • Included: Formulas and variable definitions, presets, language, theme, button vibration, and calculator display settings.
  • Not included: ANS, HISTORY, memory, the on-screen Log, unfinished input, and regular calculator function settings (DEG/RAD and auto-close after input).

Each formula’s (template’s) angle unit is included in its formula definition.

Some data is not included in backups. If you want to keep a calculation result, save it as a preset from HISTORY. It can then be included in your backup.

Your PRO purchase information is managed by the App Store or Google Play. Restoring a CalcPad backup does not affect your PRO purchase. If you have purchased PRO but cannot use its features, sign in with the same store account and try “Restore purchases” on the PRO screen.

11.2 Creating a backup

  1. Tap “Save backup.”
  2. Choose a sharing or save destination and save the JSON file.
  3. Check the destination in your files app or cloud storage to make sure the file was actually saved.

11.3 Restoring a backup

  1. If needed, back up your current formulas, presets, and settings first.
  2. Tap “Restore from backup.”
  3. Read the “Restore (overwrite)” confirmation and choose “Restore.”
  4. Select your saved backup file.
Restoring completely overwrites the target data

Restoring a backup replaces your current formulas, presets, and included settings with those in the file. It does not add to or merge with them, and the change cannot be undone. ANS, HISTORY, and memory are not restored from this backup.

CalcPad can also read backups made with older versions when their format is supported. Files from other apps and unsupported formats cannot be restored. If restoration fails, check the message on screen. Avoid editing the backup file directly.

13. Problems saving or loading data

13.1 When data cannot be saved

If ANS + HISTORY or memory cannot be saved, an unsaved-data warning appears below the header on the home and HISTORY screens. You can still use the latest available values for calculations and history operations while the warning is displayed.

  • “Retry”: Attempts to save the latest contents of the affected data again.
  • “Ignore”: Hides the warning for that data until you restart the app. It does not mean the data was saved. Automatic saving continues.

If you close the app before your changes have been saved, the latest values may be lost. Saving is not always completed when the app closes. For important values, try “Retry” while the warning is visible to check whether they can be saved.

13.2 Errors loading ANS, HISTORY, or memory

If there is a problem reading the saved ANS, HISTORY, or memory data, a recovery dialog appears. When recovery is possible, tap OK to rebuild the storage while preserving usable data where possible. Data that cannot be recovered is reset. Errors outside the supported recovery cases do not trigger a forced reset. Check the message before continuing.

14. Data migration and recovery after upgrading

This chapter explains what happens to your data when upgrading from a version earlier than 2.0.0. If you installed CalcPad for the first time with version 2.0.0 or later, you normally do not need these steps.

14.1 What happens to data when you upgrade

Your existing presets and formulas are moved to the new format during the update. Once the process finishes successfully, you can continue using them. The old copy history is not carried over to the new HISTORY and is deleted by this update. The new HISTORY records calculations completed after the update.

14.2 When older presets or formulas cannot be migrated

If presets or formulas cannot be moved to the new format, you may see an error message in the affected list. When CalcPad confirms it is safe to start again, the following options are available.

  1. Retry: First try migrating the original data again.
  2. Start with samples: Replaces only the affected group (presets or formulas) with the samples. You can start with 3 presets or 2 formulas.
  3. Start empty: Clears only the affected group (presets or formulas), so you can register new items from scratch.

For the last two options, the confirmation dialog shows the affected group and item count. Nothing is deleted until you tap “Discard legacy data and start.” Canceling leaves the data as it is.

Deleted legacy data cannot be restored

Only the legacy presets or formulas in the affected group are deleted. The other group and your settings stay unchanged. If there is anything you want to keep, review the situation before choosing to discard the data.

If CalcPad cannot safely identify the cause or what would be deleted, the discard option is unavailable. This can happen with temporary read/write errors or an unknown data format. Discarding data is not a way to resolve every storage error. Follow the on-screen guidance to try again or restart the app.

The message “New data is available. Legacy data cleanup is pending and will be retried safely at the next launch.” means CalcPad will safely try the remaining cleanup again the next time it starts.

15. Settings and usage notes

15.1 Language, theme, and vibration

In Settings, you can change Language, Appearance, and Button vibration. Japanese, English, French, Korean, and Spanish are supported, and Appearance can be set to System, Light, or Dark. For decimal and grouping separators, also check “Calculator display settings.”

15.2 Importing samples again

Using “Import sample data” in Settings replaces both your current presets and formulas with the samples. Your settings are kept. This is different from the first-time sample import or restarting just one group after a migration error. Check the confirmation before proceeding.

15.3 Input errors and calculation limits

  • Parentheses and absolute-value delimiters need to be closed for a calculation to work. For example, an unfinished expression such as sin(30 cannot be calculated.
  • Some calculations, including 1 ÷ 0, √(-1), and log(0), cannot be performed.
  • Number size or expression complexity may reach safety limits that prevent calculation or saving.
  • Approximate calculations and rounded results have limits to their precision. For important uses, check the input values, units, rounding method, and result.

15.4 Protecting your data

Uninstalling CalcPad removes the data stored on your device. It is a good idea to regularly save your formulas, presets, and settings to a file using the PRO backup feature, and check that the file is in the intended location. ANS, HISTORY, and memory are not included in backups. To keep an important result, save it as a preset from HISTORY so it can be backed up.
